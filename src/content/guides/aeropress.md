intro:
  - >-
    The AeroPress is the most forgiving brewer most people will own. Coffee steeps
    in a sealed chamber and is then pressed through a filter, so the brew is short,
    the clean-up takes seconds and almost every mistake can be corrected in the cup.
  - >-
    It suits anyone who wants good coffee without a ritual: one or two cups at a
    time, on a kitchen counter or in a hotel room. The paper filter keeps the cup
    clean and free of sediment, while the short steep and gentle pressure give it
    more body than a pour-over.
  - >-
    The limits are worth knowing. It brews one drink at a time, so it is not the
    brewer for a table of four. It is also not an espresso machine — the pressure
    from your arm is a fraction of what a pump produces, so a shorter, stronger
    brew is a concentrate rather than a shot.

keyPoint: >-
  Press slowly. Forcing the plunger does not extract more coffee; it pushes water
  around the bed and through the weakest point, which is where harsh flavours come from.

equipmentEssential:
  - name: AeroPress
    note: Chamber, plunger, filter cap and the rubber seal on the plunger.
  - name: Paper filters
    note: One per brew. They catch fines and oils, giving a clean cup.
  - name: Burr grinder
    note: Even particles matter more here than any other variable you control.
  - name: Digital scale
    note: Weighing coffee and water is what makes a good brew repeatable.
  - name: Kettle
    note: Any kettle works. You do not need a gooseneck for this method.
  - name: Sturdy mug
    note: It has to take the weight of the press without sliding or tipping.

equipmentOptional:
  - name: Timer
    note: Your phone or the timer on a scale is enough to track the steep.
  - name: Metal filter
    note: Lets more oils through for a heavier cup. Reusable, slightly more sediment.
  - name: Stirrer or spoon
    note: A short stir after the pour helps wet every ground evenly.
  - name: Travel cap
    note: Keeps the chamber sealed in a bag, which is most of the appeal.

howItWorks:
  - >-
    The AeroPress combines two things. First it is an immersion brewer: coffee and
    water sit together in the chamber, so every ground is in contact with water for
    the whole steep. That is why the method is consistent — there is no bed for
    water to channel through and no drawdown to stall.
  - >-
    Then it becomes a press. Pushing the plunger builds gentle pressure and forces
    the brew through the filter. That pressure is modest, roughly what your arm can
    apply against a small filter area, and it mostly shortens the time the brew
    takes to leave the chamber rather than driving extraction.
  - >-
    This matters for what you change. Because extraction happens during the steep,
    grind size and steep time are your main controls. Pressing harder or faster does
    not extract more; it only risks pushing water unevenly through the coffee. If
    the cup is not right, adjust what happened before the plunger moved.

technique:
  - action: Boil the kettle and rinse the filter in the cap.
    why: >-
      Rinsing removes papery flavour and warms the chamber, so the brew does not lose
      heat in the first few seconds.
  - action: Grind 15 g of coffee at a medium-fine setting.
    why: >-
      Slightly finer than pour-over. Fine enough to extract in a short steep, coarse
      enough that the press does not become a struggle.
  - action: Assemble the AeroPress on the mug and add the coffee.
    why: >-
      Check the mug is stable and wide enough to take the brewer squarely. A wobbling
      mug is the main way this method goes wrong.
  - action: Pour 220 ml of water at 90–95°C, filling the chamber steadily.
    why: >-
      Pour with enough energy to wet all the grounds. Dry pockets extract far less
      than the coffee around them.
  - action: Stir gently two or three times, then fit the plunger.
    why: >-
      The stir breaks up the crust so every ground is submerged. Fitting the plunger
      without pressing seals the chamber and slows heat loss.
  - action: Steep for four minutes.
    why: >-
      This is where extraction happens. A shorter steep gives a lighter, sharper cup;
      a longer one gives more body and more bitterness.
  - action: Press down slowly, taking 20–30 seconds to reach the bed.
    why: >-
      Slow pressure keeps the flow even. If it becomes hard work, the grind is too
      fine — ease off rather than forcing it.
  - action: Stop as soon as you hear the hiss of air.
    why: >-
      Pressing past that point pushes the last, most bitter liquid through and adds
      nothing worth drinking.
  - action: Eject the puck and rinse the seal.
    why: >-
      Coffee oils on the rubber seal turn stale quickly, and a clean seal presses more
      smoothly next time.

variables:
  - name: Grind size
    effect: >-
      Finer grinds extract faster and give more body; coarser grinds give a lighter,
      cleaner cup and press more easily.
    when: >-
      Adjust this first when the coffee tastes sharp and thin, or heavy and drying.
  - name: Steep time
    effect: >-
      Longer steeps pull more from the coffee — more sweetness at first, then more
      bitterness.
    when: >-
      Change it when the grind is right but the cup is slightly under- or over-developed.
  - name: Ratio
    effect: >-
      More coffee to the same water makes a stronger drink; more water makes it lighter.
    when: >-
      Use it when the cup is balanced but simply too strong or too weak for you.
  - name: Water temperature
    effect: >-
      Hotter water extracts faster. Cooler water softens harshness in darker roasts.
    when: >-
      Reach for it after grind and time are settled, not before.
  - name: Filter choice
    effect: >-
      Paper gives a cleaner, lighter cup. Metal lets oils through for a heavier one.
    when: >-
      Change it when you like the flavour but want a different texture.

tasteAdjustments:
  - result: Sour, thin or hollow
    cause: Under-extraction — the water did not take enough from the coffee
    change: Grind one step finer
  - result: Bitter, harsh or drying
    cause: Over-extraction, or pressing too hard at the end
    change: Grind one step coarser
  - result: Too strong, but balanced
    cause: The concentration is too high for your taste
    change: Add hot water to the cup
  - result: Too weak, but balanced
    cause: The concentration is too low
    change: Use 2 g more coffee at the same water
  - result: Hard to press
    cause: The grind is too fine for the filter
    change: Grind coarser and press more slowly

choosingCoffee:
  - >-
    The AeroPress handles almost anything, which is why it is such a good brewer for
    exploring. Medium roasts are the easiest starting point: they extract readily in a
    short steep and forgive small errors in grind and timing.
  - >-
    Light roasts work well and often taste clearer here than in a French press, though
    they may want a slightly finer grind or hotter water to open up. Dark roasts give a
    heavy, chocolate-led cup, but they extract quickly — a cooler pour and a shorter
    steep keep them from turning bitter.
  - >-
    For milk drinks, brew a concentrate: the same 15 g with roughly half the water, then
    top with hot milk. It will not behave like espresso, but it holds its character
    better than a full-length brew does.

troubleshooting:
  - problem: The coffee tastes sour and watery
    cause: The steep was too short or the grind too coarse for the time
    first: Grind one step finer and keep everything else the same
    next: If it is still sour, extend the steep by 30 seconds
  - problem: The coffee tastes bitter and dries the mouth
    cause: Too much extraction, often from a fine grind plus a hard press
    first: Grind one step coarser
    next: Press more slowly and stop at the hiss rather than pushing through it
  - problem: The plunger is very hard to push
    cause: The grind is too fine, or the filter is blocked by fines
    first: Move the grinder coarser by one or two steps
    next: Check the filter sits flat in the cap and the seal is clean
  - problem: Water leaks from the cap while steeping
    cause: The cap is not seated squarely, or the chamber rim has grounds on it
    first: Wipe the rim and refit the cap until it clicks all round
    next: Check the filter is centred and not folded at the edge
  - problem: The cup tastes flat and papery
    cause: The filter was not rinsed, or the water is heavily chlorinated
    first: Rinse the filter with hot water before adding coffee
    next: Try filtered water and compare the two cups side by side
  - problem: Every brew tastes slightly different
    cause: Something is being estimated rather than measured
    first: Weigh both the coffee and the water
    next: Fix the steep time and change only one variable between brews

cleaning:
  - >-
    After each brew, eject the puck straight into the bin, rinse the chamber and wipe
    the rubber seal. The seal is where oils collect fastest, and a sticky seal makes the
    next press feel rough long before it starts affecting the flavour.
  - >-
    Every week or so, take the plunger seal off and wash both parts in warm soapy water.
    Rinse the filter cap thoroughly — the small holes trap grounds that go stale and add
    a dull, old-coffee note to an otherwise clean cup.
  - >-
    The rubber seal is a wear part. When it hardens, loses its shine or stops sliding
    smoothly, replace it. A tired seal lets air past during the press, which is often
    mistaken for a grind problem.
