intro:
  - >-
    The V60 is a cone-shaped dripper with a spiral rib pattern and one large hole at
    the bottom. That open design hands you the controls: how fast water moves through
    the coffee is decided almost entirely by your grind and your pouring, not by the
    brewer.
  - >-
    The reward is clarity. A paper filter holds back oils and fine particles, so the
    cup is light in body and the individual flavours stay separate. It is the method
    that shows what a carefully grown, lightly roasted coffee can actually do — and
    the one most likely to make a good single origin taste like the notes on the bag.
  - >-
    The trade-off is that it asks for attention. Pour carelessly and the coffee bed
    goes uneven; grind badly and the drawdown stalls. It also brews one or two cups
    at a time. If you want to walk away mid-brew, this is not the method.

keyPoint: >-
  Keep the coffee bed flat. Most V60 problems are not grind problems — they are water
  finding an easier route through a bed that was disturbed by an uneven pour.

equipmentEssential:
  - name: V60 dripper
    note: Size 01 for one cup, size 02 for one or two. Plastic, ceramic or glass all work.
  - name: V60 paper filters
    note: Cone filters sized to the dripper. A folded flat-bottom filter will not seat properly.
  - name: Burr grinder
    note: Even particles keep the flow predictable. This matters more here than in immersion brewing.
  - name: Digital scale
    note: Weigh the coffee and the water as it pours. Volume measures are not precise enough.
  - name: Kettle
    note: Any kettle brews good V60. A gooseneck helps control, but it is not required.
  - name: Carafe or mug
    note: Whatever the dripper sits on must be stable and hold the full brew volume.

equipmentOptional:
  - name: Gooseneck kettle
    note: A narrow spout makes slow, placed pours easier to repeat.
  - name: Timer

  - name: Thermometer
    note: Helpful while you learn how far your kettle drops off the boil.
  - name: Glass server
    note: Lets you see the brew volume and swirl before pouring.

howItWorks:
  - >-
    The V60 is percolation brewing: water passes through a bed of coffee once and
    leaves. Gravity does the work, and the coffee is never fully submerged for long.
    Each part of the bed is extracted by the water travelling past it, so how evenly
    that water is distributed decides how evenly the coffee extracts.
  - >-
    The cone shape and single large opening mean the brewer imposes very little. A
    flat-bottomed brewer with small holes restricts flow and evens things out for you.
    The V60 does not, which is why the same coffee can taste clean and sweet one
    morning and thin and sharp the next.
  - >-
    Two forces set the contact time. Grind size controls how tightly the bed packs and
    how easily water moves through it; pouring controls how much water sits above the
    bed at any moment. Fine grinds and high water levels both slow the brew and raise
    extraction. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why almost every fix on this
    page is a grind change or a pouring change.

technique:
  - action: Boil the kettle and let it settle for about 30 seconds.
    why: >-
      Water straight off a rolling boil is hotter than most recipes intend, particularly
      with a darker roast.
  - action: Put the filter in the dripper and rinse it thoroughly with hot water.
    why: >-
      Rinsing washes out papery flavour and warms the dripper and carafe, so the brew
      does not lose several degrees in the first pour. Discard the rinse water.
  - action: Grind 15 g of coffee at a medium setting and level the bed.
    why: >-
      A flat starting bed is the easiest way to get an even one. Give the dripper a
      gentle shake rather than pressing the coffee down.
  - action: Pour 45–50 g of water and wait 30–45 seconds.
    why: >-
      This is the bloom. Fresh coffee releases carbon dioxide that repels water, and
      letting it escape first means the remaining water can wet the bed evenly.
  - action: Pour slowly in small circles until the scale reads about 150 g.
    why: >-
      Keep the stream inside the coffee, away from the paper. Water that runs down the
      filter wall bypasses the bed entirely and dilutes the cup.
  - action: Pour the remaining water to 250 g, keeping the level roughly constant.
    why: >-
      Steady pouring keeps the contact time predictable. Dumping water in raises the
      level, slows the drawdown and over-extracts.
  - action: Give the dripper one gentle swirl and let it drain.
    why: >-
      A swirl settles grounds off the filter wall and flattens the bed again, which
      makes the last part of the brew far more even.
  - action: Aim for the drawdown to finish around 3:00–3:30 from the first pour.
    why: >-
      Time is evidence rather than a target. A brew that finishes very early or very
      late tells you where to look before you have even tasted it.
  - action: Swirl the carafe and let the coffee cool for a minute before judging it.
    why: >-
      The first and last liquid through are not the same strength, and very hot coffee
      hides both sweetness and faults.

variables:
  - name: Grind size
    effect: >-
      Finer grinds slow the drawdown and raise extraction; coarser grinds speed it up
      and lighten the cup.
    when: >-
      This is the first thing to change when a cup is sour and fast, or drying and slow.
  - name: Pouring technique
    effect: >-
      Slow, central, circular pours keep the bed even. Fast or edge-heavy pours create
      channels and bypass.
    when: >-
      Reach for it when the drawdown time looks normal but the cup tastes both sour and harsh.
  - name: Water temperature
    effect: >-
      Hotter water extracts more from dense, lightly roasted coffee. Cooler water softens
      a dark roast.
    when: >-
      Adjust after the grind is settled, especially with very light or very dark coffee.
  - name: Ratio
    effect: >-
      More coffee to the same water gives a stronger cup without changing how it extracts.
    when: >-
      Use it when the balance is right but the cup is simply too strong or too weak.
  - name: Bloom length
    effect: >-
      A longer bloom releases more gas from very fresh coffee and steadies the pours
      that follow.
    when: >-
      Extend it when the bed bubbles vigorously and the brew feels unpredictable.

tasteAdjustments:
  - result: Sour, thin or hollow
    cause: Under-extraction — the water passed through too quickly
    change: Grind one step finer
  - result: Bitter, dry or astringent
    cause: Over-extraction, often from a slow drawdown
    change: Grind one step coarser
  - result: Sour and harsh at once
    cause: Uneven extraction — water bypassed part of the bed
    change: Pour more slowly in smaller circles, away from the filter wall
  - result: Too strong, but balanced
    cause: The concentration is too high
    change: Brew the same coffee with 20–30 ml more water
  - result: Too weak, but balanced
    cause: The concentration is too low
    change: Use 1–2 g more coffee at the same water
  - result: Flat and dull
    cause: The filter was not rinsed, or the brewing water is heavily chlorinated
    change: Rinse the paper properly and try filtered water

choosingCoffee:
  - >-
    The V60 flatters coffee with something to say. Light and medium roasts are the
    natural fit: the clean filter and short contact time let floral, citrus and stone
    fruit character come through, which is exactly what gets lost in a heavier brew.
  - >-
    Washed coffees tend to taste especially clear here, since the method separates
    flavours rather than blending them. Naturals work well too and bring more fruit and
    sweetness, though they can taste boozy if the grind runs fine and the drawdown drags.
  - >-
    Dark roasts are not off the table, but they are the hardest to get right. They are
    more soluble, so they over-extract quickly — go a step coarser and use water at the
    lower end of the range. The V60 is not built for milk drinks: the body is too light
    to hold up, and a moka pot or espresso will serve you better there.

troubleshooting:
  - problem: The drawdown stalls and the water sits on top
    cause: The grind is too fine, or fines have blocked the filter
    first: Move the grinder one or two steps coarser
    next: Pour more gently and stop stirring the bed, which drives fines downwards
  - problem: The brew finishes far too quickly
    cause: The grind is too coarse for the dose, or water ran down the filter wall
    first: Grind one step finer
    next: Keep the pour inside the coffee bed, at least a centimetre from the paper
  - problem: The cup tastes sour even at a normal brew time
    cause: The coffee is dense or lightly roasted and needs more energy
    first: Use water at the top of the range, around 96°C
    next: Grind slightly finer, or extend the bloom to 45 seconds
  - problem: The cup dries the mouth
    cause: Astringency from uneven extraction rather than simple over-extraction
    first: Pour in slower, smaller circles and swirl once at the end
    next: Check the bed is flat before the first pour and after the last
  - problem: A crater or hole appears in the coffee bed
    cause: The pour was too forceful or landed in one spot
    first: Lower the kettle closer to the bed and pour more softly
    next: Move in wider, slower circles rather than pouring into the centre
  - problem: Grounds are stuck high on the filter wall
    cause: The pour pushed coffee up the paper, where it stops extracting
    first: Swirl the dripper gently after the last pour
    next: Keep the water level lower so the bed does not ride up the filter

cleaning:
  - >-
    After each brew, bin the paper and grounds together and rinse the dripper under hot
    water. Plastic and ceramic cones both hold onto coffee oils around the ribs, which
    is where a stale, slightly rancid smell starts.
  - >-
    Every week, wash the dripper and carafe with warm soapy water and pay attention to
    the rim and the ribs. Glass and ceramic servers pick up a brown film that is easy to
    stop noticing and impossible to ignore once the coffee starts tasting dull.
  - >-
    Store paper filters somewhere dry and closed. They absorb kitchen smells readily,
    and a filter that has been sitting open next to spices will pass that on to the cup
    no matter how well you rinse it.
