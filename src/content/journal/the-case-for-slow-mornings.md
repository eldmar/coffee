There is a version of coffee that is fuel: a pod, a button, a cup in forty seconds. It works. But if that's the only coffee you know, you're missing what the slow version is actually for.

## The ritual is the point

A pour over takes about four minutes of your full attention: the bloom, the spiral pours, the drawdown. You cannot scroll a feed with a gooseneck kettle in your hand. That's not an inconvenience — it's the feature. Four minutes where your hands are busy and your mind is allowed to be quiet.

## You learn to taste

Brewing manually gives you knobs to turn, and turning them teaches you what the words on the bag mean. Grind a little finer and taste what "body" is. Pour a little hotter and find "brightness." Nobody learns to taste from a pod machine, because nothing ever changes.

## It scales down, not just up

The slow morning doesn't require a shelf of gear. One dripper, one filter, one kettle — the whole kit costs less than a month of café visits and lives in a drawer. The luxury isn't the equipment. It's the ten minutes.

Start with one slow morning a week. Sunday is traditional. See what happens to the other six.
