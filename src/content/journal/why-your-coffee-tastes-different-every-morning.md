title: Why your coffee tastes different every morning

You bought good beans. You followed the same recipe as yesterday. And yet today's cup tastes flat where yesterday's sang. Before you blame the roaster, look at four variables that drift without telling you.

## Your grinder is aging faster than you think

Burrs dull slowly, and dull burrs produce more fines — tiny particles that over-extract and taste bitter. But day to day, the bigger culprit is static and retention: a gram of yesterday's grounds hiding in the chute, landing in today's dose. Purge a few grams before dosing and the mystery often disappears.

## Water is half the recipe

If you brew with tap water, you brew with whatever the city sent you that day. Hardness and chlorine both move. Filtered water — even a basic pitcher filter — removes the biggest daily swing you can't taste until it's in the cup.

## Coffee ages on a curve

Beans are not stable. A bag roasted two weeks ago brews differently than the same bag at five weeks — gas escapes, aromatics fade, extraction speeds up. If your cup slowly went from bright to dull over a month, that's not your technique. Buy smaller bags more often.

## The dose creeps

"About two scoops" can drift by two grams in a week without you noticing, and two grams is a visible change in strength. A £15 scale ends the guessing permanently. Weigh the coffee, weigh the water, and suddenly your mornings agree with each other.

The takeaway: pick one variable, fix it for a week, and taste. Consistency isn't a talent — it's the removal of drift.
