## The short answer

A **Cappuccino** combines espresso with more aerated milk, producing a light, velvety drink with a noticeable foam structure. A **Flat White** uses thinner, glossier microfoam that integrates closely with the espresso, so the coffee usually tastes more direct.

Both can use the same double shot and a similar 150–180 ml cup. Their clearest difference is not caffeine but how much air enters the milk and how that texture changes each sip.

## Espresso Base

Both drinks should begin with balanced espresso. KAVOVO uses 18 g dry coffee to produce 36–40 g espresso, but the exact dose should suit the basket in your machine.

The [Cappuccino recipe](/recipes/cappuccino/) and [Flat White recipe](/recipes/flat-white/) therefore share a coffee foundation. If one tastes more powerful, milk balance and texture are creating that impression rather than a hidden extra shot.

Taste the espresso before steaming. Sourness, harsh bitterness or channeling remain present under milk even when sweetness makes them less obvious. The [Espresso brew guide](/guides/espresso/) covers the base extraction.

## Milk Quantity

The recipes overlap, but a Flat White generally uses a little less milk. Around 110–130 ml before steaming fits a compact Flat White; a Cappuccino may begin with 120–150 ml because extra aeration expands the milk further.

Pitcher leftovers make comparison difficult. Weigh or measure the milk while learning, then pour only what the cup needs. Filling a 300 ml mug changes either drink into a much milkier balance regardless of its name.

## Microfoam and Texture

This is the defining difference. Cappuccino milk receives more air during the first stage of steaming. The bubbles must remain extremely fine, but the finished volume is visibly expanded and feels lighter on the palate.

Flat White milk receives less air. The goal is a thin microfoam distributed through the whole pitcher, with a surface that looks glossy and fluid. It should flow into the espresso rather than sitting above it as a separate cap.

Neither drink should contain large bubbles or stiff spoonable foam. Modern Cappuccino texture is velvety, not a dry mountain. The [Steam Silky Milk lesson](/learn/dial-in-espresso/steam-silky-milk/) explains stretching, rolling and temperature in detail.

## Typical Cup Size

A 150–180 ml cup works for both KAVOVO recipes. Within that range, the Cappuccino feels fuller because aerated milk occupies more volume, while the Flat White feels denser and more integrated.

Some cafés serve Cappuccino in a slightly larger cup or Flat White in a smaller one. Names vary internationally, so cup size is a useful clue but not a complete definition. Look at espresso dose, milk quantity and texture together.

## Which Tastes Stronger?

A Flat White usually tastes more coffee-forward because it uses slightly less milk and less incorporated air. Its silky texture carries espresso across the whole sip.

A Cappuccino can still taste intense, especially with a medium-dark espresso blend. Its expanded foam changes aroma and mouthfeel, and the greater milk volume softens the coffee. “Stronger” here means perceived concentration, not caffeine.

## Which Has More Caffeine?

They contain the same caffeine when they use the same coffee dose and espresso yield. Steaming more milk does not add or remove caffeine.

A café may use one shot in a Cappuccino and two in a Flat White, or the reverse, so ask about shots when caffeine matters. Cup size alone cannot answer the question.

## How to Make Each Drink at Home

For either drink, pull a consistent double espresso and begin with very cold milk in a clean pitcher.

For Cappuccino, introduce a gentle paper-tearing sound for several seconds, then lower the wand slightly to roll the milk until 60–65°C. The pitcher should expand more while staying glossy.

For Flat White, introduce air only briefly, establish the rolling vortex earlier and stop around 55–60°C. Tap out visible bubbles, swirl and pour immediately from low height so the milk integrates.

Do not try to make two textures from one pitcher. Steam each drink separately while learning; the extra minute is more useful than pouring a compromise that resembles neither.

## Which Should You Choose?

Choose Cappuccino when you enjoy an airy, velvety texture and a balanced meeting of espresso, warm milk and foam. Choose Flat White when you want a silkier, denser drink where espresso remains more prominent.

If you want a much larger and softer drink, compare the Flat White with a Latte in the broader [Cortado vs Flat White vs Latte guide](/learn/coffee-basics/cortado-vs-flat-white-vs-latte/). This page stays focused on the closer Cappuccino and Flat White distinction.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the main difference between a Cappuccino and Flat White?

The main difference is milk texture. A Cappuccino uses more aeration for noticeable velvety foam, while a Flat White uses a thin layer of glossy microfoam integrated with the espresso.

### Which is stronger, Cappuccino or Flat White?

A Flat White usually tastes stronger because it often uses slightly less milk and less air. Caffeine can be identical when both use the same double espresso.

### Does a Cappuccino have more foam than a Flat White?

Yes. A Cappuccino has a more expanded, noticeable foam structure. The bubbles should still be fine and glossy rather than large, dry or stiff.

### Which drink has more caffeine?

Neither automatically has more. Caffeine depends on the coffee dose and number of espresso shots, not the amount of steamed milk or foam.

### Are Cappuccino and Flat White cups the same size?

They often overlap around 150–180 ml. Some cafés vary the sizes, so espresso dose and milk texture are more reliable distinctions than cup volume alone.

### Which milk is best for both drinks?

Cold whole milk is forgiving and naturally sweet. A barista-style oat drink can also work well. Technique matters more than brand: build a stable vortex and stop before the milk becomes too hot.
