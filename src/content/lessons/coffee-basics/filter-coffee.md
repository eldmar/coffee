## The short answer

To make balanced filter coffee, start with **15 g coffee and 250 ml water**, a medium grind and water at approximately **92-96°C**. Rinse the paper filter, wet all the grounds evenly, then let the water pass through without rushing the brew.

The same starting ratio works for a V60, Chemex or automatic drip machine. Change the grind before changing several other variables at once.

</div>

## What Is Filter Coffee?

Filter coffee is brewed when water passes through a bed of ground coffee and then through a paper, metal or cloth filter. The filter separates the drink from the grounds while the water carries soluble flavour into the cup.

The term covers several methods. A V60 and Chemex are manual pour-over brewers, while an automatic drip machine controls the pouring for you. All produce a cleaner cup than an unfiltered immersion method because the filter holds back fine particles and some coffee oils.

Filter coffee is not the same as an Americano. An [Americano](/recipes/americano/) is espresso diluted with hot water; filter coffee extracts the full drink directly through the coffee bed.

## Best Coffee-to-Water Ratio for Filter Coffee

A useful starting point is **60 g coffee per litre of water**, or approximately **1:16.7**. Use the [coffee-to-water ratio calculator](/learn/coffee-basics/coffee-to-water-ratio/) when you want to scale a different dose.

| Coffee | Water | Approximate yield |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 15 g | 250 ml | 1 mug |
| 30 g | 500 ml | 2 mugs |
| 45 g | 750 ml | 3 mugs |
| 60 g | 1 litre | 4 mugs |

For a stronger cup, move towards 1:15. For a lighter cup, try 1:17. Ratio changes concentration; it does not fix poor extraction by itself.

## Best Grind Size for Filter Coffee

Start with a medium grind, similar in texture to table salt. The exact setting depends on the brewer, filter, batch size and grinder, so use brew time and taste as feedback.

- Grind slightly finer when the coffee runs quickly and tastes sour, thin or underdeveloped.
- Grind slightly coarser when the brew stalls and tastes bitter, dry or hollow.
- Use a somewhat coarser grind for a large Chemex batch than for a small V60.

The [coffee grind size guide](/learn/coffee-basics/grind-size/) explains why small changes affect flow and extraction.

## Filter Coffee Methods

### V60 pour over

A V60 gives you direct control over pouring, agitation and brew time. It is a good choice for a clear, aromatic cup and for learning how small recipe changes affect taste. Follow the [V60 Pour Over recipe](/recipes/v60-pour-over/) for a timed method.

### Automatic drip machine

An automatic machine heats and distributes the water for you. Weigh the coffee and water, use a clean basket and check that the machine reaches a suitable brewing temperature. A consistent machine can make excellent filter coffee with much less hands-on work.

### Chemex

Chemex uses thick paper filters that create a particularly clean cup. Rinse the paper thoroughly and grind slightly coarser than for a small V60 so a larger batch does not drain too slowly.

### AeroPress hybrid

An AeroPress is primarily an immersion brewer, but recipes that use a paper filter produce a clean cup with some filter-coffee character. Start with the [AeroPress Daily recipe](/recipes/aeropress-daily/) and dilute only when the result is more concentrated than you prefer.

### French Press comparison

A [Classic French Press](/recipes/classic-french-press/) steeps coffee in water before a metal mesh separates the grounds. More oils and fine particles remain in the cup, giving it a fuller body than paper-filtered coffee.

## Basic Filter Coffee Recipe

For one mug, use **15 g coffee**, **250 ml filtered water**, a medium grind and water at **92-96°C**.

1. Heat the water and rinse the paper filter with hot water.
2. Add the ground coffee and gently level the bed.
3. Place the brewer and cup or server on a scale, then tare it.
4. Pour approximately 45 ml of water, wetting all the coffee, and wait 30-45 seconds.
5. Continue pouring slowly and evenly until the scale reaches 250 ml.
6. Let the water drain through. Aim for a total brew time of roughly 2:30-3:30.
7. Remove the brewer, swirl the coffee and taste it as it cools.

Use fresh coffee, filtered water and clean equipment. For roast-specific starting points, see [coffee brewing temperature](/learn/coffee-basics/brewing-temperature/) and the guide to [coffee freshness and roast dates](/learn/understand-your-beans/coffee-freshness-roast-dates/).

## Filter Coffee vs Americano

| Feature | Filter Coffee | Americano |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brewing method | Water passes through coffee | Espresso diluted with water |
| Texture | Clean and light | More concentrated |
| Crema | No | May be present |
| Typical size | 200–350 ml | 150–300 ml |

Neither is automatically stronger. The dose, final drink size and recipe determine concentration, while the brewing method changes texture and flavour.

## Common Filter Coffee Problems

### The coffee tastes weak or watery

Use a little more coffee or less water, but check the brew time too. A very fast brew may need a finer grind rather than a dramatically stronger ratio.

### The coffee tastes bitter or dry

Grind slightly coarser, shorten an excessively long brew or use slightly cooler water for a dark roast. Change one variable and brew again.

### The coffee tastes sour

Try a slightly finer grind and make sure all the grounds are evenly wet. Water that is too cool can also leave light-roast coffee under-extracted.

### The water drains too quickly

Use a finer grind and pour more gently. Check that the filter sits correctly and that water is not bypassing the coffee bed around the edge.

### The water drains too slowly

Use a coarser grind, reduce aggressive stirring and avoid pouring directly onto the paper. Excess fine particles can block the filter.

### The cup tastes muddy or dry

For a muddy cup, grind coarser and pour with less agitation so fewer fine particles block the filter. For a dry finish, shorten an excessively long brew and check that the coffee is not being extracted too aggressively.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is filter coffee?

Filter coffee is made by passing hot water through ground coffee and a filter. The filter separates the brewed drink from the grounds and usually creates a clean cup with less sediment.

### What is the best filter coffee ratio?

Start with 60 g of coffee per litre of water, approximately 1:16.7. That is 15 g coffee for 250 ml water. Move towards 1:15 for a stronger cup or 1:17 for a lighter one.

### How much coffee should I use for one cup?

Use 15 g of coffee for one 250 ml cup as a balanced starting recipe. Scale both coffee and water together when your cup or batch is larger.

### What grind size is best for filter coffee?

Start with a medium grind. Grind finer if the brew runs quickly and tastes sour, or coarser if it drains slowly and tastes bitter or dry.

### Is an Americano the same as filter coffee?

No. Filter coffee is extracted as water passes through ground coffee and a filter. An Americano is made by brewing espresso first and then diluting it with hot water.

### Can I make filter coffee without a machine?

Yes. A manual V60, Chemex or other pour-over brewer needs only a filter, a kettle and a cup or server. Weighing the coffee and water makes the result easier to repeat.
