## The short answer

To dial in espresso, choose a fixed dose, set a target yield, pull a shot and record the time. Taste it, then change only the grind:

- if the shot reaches the target too quickly and tastes thin or sour, grind finer;
- if it reaches the target too slowly and tastes harsh, dry or bitter, grind coarser;
- if the flow looks uneven or the cup tastes both sour and bitter, improve puck preparation before chasing the grinder.

Repeat in small steps until the espresso tastes balanced. The aim is not to hit 30 seconds. The aim is to find a recipe worth repeating.

</div>

## Before the first shot

Espresso equipment needs to be stable before it can be predictable.

1. Let the machine, group head and portafilter heat fully. Follow the warm-up time in the machine manual.
2. Fill the water tank with suitable water and make sure the drip tray has space.
3. Use coffee that is not extremely old or straight from the roaster while still releasing large amounts of gas.
4. Clean and dry the basket.
5. Put a scale and timer in position.

If the machine is cold for shot one and fully hot for shot three, you are not comparing the same conditions.

## Step 1: choose a dose for the basket

Check the basket's intended capacity. For an 18 g basket, start with 18 g. Keep this dose fixed throughout the initial dial-in.

Weigh the dose after grinding. Grinder timers are useful once calibrated, but retained grounds and changes in bean density can alter the actual output.

## Step 2: choose a starting yield

Start at a 1:2 brew ratio unless the roaster provides a different recipe.

For example:

- dose: 18 g;
- target yield: 36 g;
- initial time window: roughly 25–35 seconds from pump start.

Place the cup on the scale, tare it and stop the pump just before the display reaches the target. A little liquid may continue to drip after the pump stops, so learn the small offset your machine needs.

## Step 3: prepare the puck consistently

Break up obvious clumps, distribute the grounds evenly and tamp level. Do the same thing for every shot.

Perfectly polished choreography is unnecessary. What matters is an even coffee bed without gaps, cracks or a tilted surface. More force after the puck is fully compressed will not rescue poor distribution.

## Step 4: pull and record the first shot

Record:

- dose;
- yield;
- time;
- grinder setting;
- taste;
- any obvious flow problem.

A simple note is enough:

> 18 in · 36 out · 21 s · thin and sharply sour

Do not change the recipe halfway through because the stream looks dramatic. Stop at the planned yield, then assess the complete cup.

## Step 5: make the first grind adjustment

Use time and taste together.

| Result | Likely next move |
| --- | --- |
| Target yield arrives very fast; shot is thin or sharply sour | Grind finer |
| Target yield arrives very slowly; shot is harsh, dry or bitter | Grind coarser |
| Time looks reasonable; shot is still sour | Try a slightly finer grind or a slightly longer yield after confirming even flow |
| Time looks reasonable; shot is bitter and drying | Try a slightly coarser grind or a slightly shorter yield |
| Shot sprays, starts from one side or tastes sour and bitter | Fix distribution and tamping first |

Adjust in small increments. Espresso grind settings are sensitive, and the useful difference may be one click or less.

Follow the grinder manual when adjusting. Some grinders should be running when moving finer. After a change, purge only enough coffee to remove grounds retained at the old setting.

## Step 6: pull the next shot with everything else unchanged

Use the same dose, yield, temperature, pre-infusion and puck-preparation method. If you change dose, grind and yield together, you will not know which change helped.

Compare the new shot with the previous one. If it moved in the right direction, make another small change. If it moved past the balanced point, step back slightly.

## Step 7: refine by taste

Once the shot reaches the target yield in a sensible time, stop treating the time window as a finish line.

- For more extraction and a more open flavour, try a slightly longer yield.
- For more concentration and less drying bitterness, try a slightly shorter yield.
- If the coffee tastes dull at every grind setting, review water, temperature and bean freshness.

Taste the espresso after stirring it. The first liquid and last liquid from a shot are not identical, and crema can taste intensely bitter on its own.

## Expect the setting to move

Coffee changes after opening, the room changes temperature and the grinder warms during use. A setting that worked three days ago may need a small correction today.

That is normal. Dialing in is not finding one permanent number; it is using the same process to find today's number quickly.

<div class="callout callout--exercise">

## Try this today

Pull three shots using one fixed dose and yield. Change only the grind between shots. Write one line for each result and place the cups in order from fastest to slowest. Taste them side by side after stirring.

The comparison will teach you more than trying to remember yesterday's espresso.

</div>

<div class="callout callout--takeaway">

## Key takeaway

Warm the equipment, fix the dose and yield, adjust grind in small steps and taste every shot. Change one variable at a time so each cup tells you something useful.
