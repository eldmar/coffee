An Americano is made by adding hot water to espresso. It keeps the flavour of espresso while creating a longer, lighter drink that is closer in strength to filter coffee.

## Steps

1. Preheat a 200–250 ml cup, then empty it.
2. Grind 18 g of coffee and brew a double espresso. Aim for a final yield of 36–40 g in approximately 25–30 seconds.
3. Add 120–180 ml of fresh hot water to the espresso.
4. Taste the drink before adding more water. Stop when the strength feels balanced rather than thin.
5. Serve immediately.

## Recommended ratio

Start with one part espresso to three parts water.

- Use less water for a stronger, more espresso-forward Americano.
- Use more water for a lighter cup, but avoid diluting it until the coffee tastes hollow.

## Americano vs Long Black

For an Americano, the espresso is normally brewed first and hot water is added afterwards. A Long Black is prepared in the opposite order: espresso is poured over hot water, helping preserve more crema and aroma.

## Hot Americano vs Iced Americano

This recipe uses fresh hot water and is served immediately in a preheated cup. The [Iced Americano recipe](/recipes/iced-americano/) uses cold water and plenty of ice, with a separate dilution table and an optional splash of milk.

## Tips

- Use fresh hot water rather than water that has been repeatedly boiled.
- Water just below boiling point is sufficient; aggressively boiling water can make the drink less pleasant to drink immediately.
- If the Americano tastes bitter before water is added, adjust the espresso rather than trying to hide the problem through dilution.
- Use a smaller cup when you want more body and intensity.
