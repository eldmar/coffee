## Steps

1. Add 20 g of dark chocolate or 20 ml chocolate syrup to a preheated cup.
2. Brew a 36–40 g double espresso directly over the chocolate.
3. Stir until the chocolate has completely melted and the mixture looks smooth.
4. Pour 160–180 ml of cold milk into a pitcher.
5. Steam the milk to approximately 60–65°C, creating a glossy texture with a thin layer of microfoam.
6. Pour the steamed milk into the espresso and chocolate mixture.
7. Finish with a light dusting of cocoa or grated chocolate and serve.

## Chocolate Syrup or Cocoa Powder?

Chocolate syrup dissolves quickly and gives a smooth, sweet Cafe Mocha. For a less processed cocoa version, mix 8 g unsweetened cocoa powder with 10 g sugar and a spoonful of hot espresso to make a paste, then stir in the remaining espresso. Dark chocolate containing 55–70% cocoa gives the richest texture with controlled sweetness.

## Mocha vs Latte

Both drinks combine espresso and steamed milk, but a mocha adds chocolate. A [Caffè Latte](/recipes/caffe-latte/) is softer and milk-led; Caffè Mocha is sweeter, fuller and often served in a similar 250–300 ml cup.

## Less-Sweet Mocha

Use 10 g of 70% dark chocolate or the unsweetened cocoa method, and do not add extra sugar until you have tasted the finished drink. Reducing chocolate rather than increasing espresso keeps the drink balanced.

## Iced Mocha Variation

Stir 20 g chocolate or 20 ml syrup into the hot double espresso, then let it cool for 30–60 seconds. Fill a tall glass with 100–120 g ice and 160 ml cold milk, pour over the chocolate espresso and stir thoroughly.

## Tips

- Dissolve the chocolate fully in hot espresso before adding milk.
- Taste the drink before adding sugar; prepared chocolate syrup may already be very sweet.
- Use coffee with chocolate, caramel or nutty flavour notes for a naturally harmonious result.
