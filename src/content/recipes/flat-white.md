## Steps

1. Preheat a 150–180 ml cup, then empty and dry it.
2. Grind 18 g of coffee and brew 36–40 g double espresso in approximately 25–30 seconds.
3. Steam 110–130 ml of cold milk to 55–60°C, adding only enough air to create thin, glossy microfoam.
4. Swirl the pitcher, then pour from low height so the milk integrates with the espresso and leaves a thin foam layer.
5. Serve immediately while the microfoam is glossy and tightly textured.

## Flat White Ingredients

Use **36–40 g double espresso**, **110–130 ml steamed milk** and a **150–180 ml cup**. The milk should have a thin layer of glossy microfoam rather than a deep, dry cap.

## How to Make a Flat White

Begin with a balanced double [espresso](/recipes/espresso/). Steam a small amount of milk with minimal aeration, swirl until it looks like wet paint and pour immediately so milk and coffee stay integrated.

## Flat White Coffee Ratio

A practical starting point is one part espresso to roughly three parts steamed milk: 36–40 g espresso with 110–130 ml milk. Cup volume and milk expansion mean the exact finished ratio varies slightly.

This makes the drink more coffee-forward than a latte while remaining smoother than straight espresso.

## Best Cup Size

Use a 150–180 ml ceramic cup. A much larger cup requires more milk, shifts the balance towards a latte and makes the espresso flavour less prominent.

## How to Steam Flat White Milk

Introduce air only during the first few seconds, then keep the steam wand tip just below the surface to create a rolling vortex. Stop at 55–60°C, tap out any visible bubbles and swirl until the milk is glossy. The [Steam Silky Milk lesson](/learn/dial-in-espresso/steam-silky-milk/) covers the technique in detail.

## Flat White vs Latte

A [Caffè Latte](/recipes/caffe-latte/) normally uses more milk and a larger cup. A Flat White is smaller, has a higher coffee-to-milk ratio and uses a thinner layer of microfoam.

The [Cortado vs Flat White vs Latte guide](/learn/coffee-basics/cortado-vs-flat-white-vs-latte/) compares all three by cup size, milk quantity and perceived strength.

## Flat White vs Cappuccino

A [Cappuccino](/recipes/cappuccino/) has a more noticeable foam layer and an airier texture. A Flat White keeps the milk silkier and more integrated with the espresso.

Read [Cappuccino vs Flat White](/learn/coffee-basics/cappuccino-vs-flat-white/) for the detailed difference in aeration, texture and caffeine.

## Flat White vs Cortado

A [Cortado](/recipes/cortado/) uses roughly equal parts espresso and steamed milk and has very little foam. A Flat White contains more milk, uses fine microfoam and is usually served in a slightly larger cup.

## Barista tips

- If the shot tastes sour, grind slightly finer; if bitter, grind coarser.
- Whole milk textures easily, but a barista oat drink can also create suitable microfoam.
- Avoid stretching the milk until it becomes thick or stiff; that texture belongs closer to a dry cappuccino.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a Flat White coffee?

A Flat White is a small espresso-and-milk drink made with a double espresso and a thin layer of silky microfoam. It is more coffee-forward than a latte.

### What is the ratio for a Flat White?

Start with 36–40 g double espresso and 110–130 ml steamed milk, approximately one part espresso to three parts milk by weight or volume.

### Is a Flat White stronger than a latte?

It usually tastes stronger because it contains a similar espresso dose in a smaller drink with less milk. The caffeine may be the same when both use a double shot.

### What size cup is used for a Flat White?

A 150–180 ml cup is a practical size. A much larger cup needs more milk and changes the drink towards a latte.

### How much foam should a Flat White have?

Only a thin, glossy layer of microfoam should sit at the surface. The milk should remain fluid and integrated rather than forming a thick, dry foam cap.
