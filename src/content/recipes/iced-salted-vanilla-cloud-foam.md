Skimmed milk creates the lightest, airiest foam. Use whole milk for a creamier texture, although the foam may be slightly denser.

## Steps

1. Grind 18 g of coffee and pull a double espresso, aiming for a final yield of 36–40 g. Leave it to cool for approximately one minute.
2. Fill a tall glass with approximately 120 g of ice. Add 150 ml of cold milk.
3. Slowly pour the espresso over the milk and ice. Pouring it over the back of a spoon can help create a more defined layered appearance.
4. Combine 60 ml of very cold milk, 10 ml of vanilla syrup and a small pinch of sea salt in a chilled frothing jug. Froth for 25–40 seconds until the mixture becomes light, glossy and approximately doubles in volume. The texture should remain pourable rather than becoming stiff.
5. Pour or spoon the salted vanilla cloud foam over the iced coffee. Serve immediately without stirring, allowing the coffee to pass through the foam while drinking.

## Troubleshooting

### The foam collapses quickly

The milk or frothing jug may not be cold enough. Use milk directly from the fridge and chill the jug before frothing.

### The foam is too dense

Reduce the frothing time or use skimmed milk instead of whole milk or cream.

### The drink tastes too salty

Use no more than a very small pinch of fine salt. Prepare a new portion of unsalted foam and mix it with the existing foam if necessary.

### The drink is too sweet

Reduce the vanilla syrup from 10 ml to 5 ml. Do not compensate by adding more salt.

## Variations

- **Dairy-free:** use a cold barista oat milk suitable for frothing.
- **Sweeter version:** add 5 ml of vanilla syrup directly to the espresso.
- **Stronger coffee:** use 45–50 g of espresso while keeping the same amount of milk.
- **Without an espresso machine:** use 50 ml of strong Moka Pot coffee.
